Abdallah MahmoudMechatronics

I like the point where a circuit stops being a diagram and starts doing something.

I'm Abdallah Mahmoud Mohammed Mohammed, twenty, studying mechatronics engineering in Egypt. Mechatronics sits between mechanical, electronics, and code — which is why I ended up learning a bit of each rather than picking one.

Most of what I know came from building things and breaking them: microcontroller projects, sensors, motors, and the code that ties them together. Python is the language I actually think in; everything else I pick up as a project demands it.

I'm early in this and I'd rather say so than pretend otherwise. But I'm comfortable reading a datasheet, debugging a circuit that refuses to behave, and explaining any of it in Arabic or English — I help other students often, and teaching is the fastest way to find out what you actually understand.

01

Embedded electronics

Microcontrollers, sensors, motor drivers, and the wiring between them. Where most of my hands-on time goes, and where things break in the most interesting ways.

02

Control & motion

Reading a sensor, deciding what to do, and moving something accordingly. Feedback loops, and the tuning that turns a twitchy machine into a steady one.

03

Code for hardware

Python for logic, scripting, and anything that talks to a board. Small tools that make a build easier to test instead of easier to demo.

04

Putting it together

Getting the board, the code, and the person using it to agree. This is the part that takes longest, and the part that teaches most.

05

Explaining things

Helping other students get unstuck, in Arabic or English. It sharpens my own understanding more than anything else I do.
